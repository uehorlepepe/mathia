1. Exploration Topic : sufficiently narrow, and plotted before committing

A Comparative Parametric and Kinematic Analysis of Underwater Hand Trajectories Between a High School Competitive Swimmer and an Elite Freestyle Sprinter

The topic is deliberately narrow: it concerns one phase of one stroke, the underwater hand pull of freestyle, for one hand, in two swimmers, rather than "modelling swimming" or "parametric curves" in general.

2. Specific Aim / Objective

What exactly are you trying to find out, and how will you know you have succeeded?

To model the 2D underwater hand-pull trajectories of my own competitive 50 m freestyle sprint and of an elite world-class sprinter as parametric polynomial functions x(t) and y(t), to compute the path length and instantaneous velocity of each hand using calculus, and to quantify how far my motion departs from the elite model using vector residuals, RMSE and the coefficient of determination R² of the elite model applied to my own data. Success will be measured by four explicit criteria:

  • Convergent models: a parametric fit to each trajectory with R² ≥ 0.95 in the pull window, whose residuals show no systematic trend.
  • Computed kinematics: an arc length and a mean and maximum hand speed for each of my two clips, each carrying an uncertainty propagated from the calibration.
  • A quantified discrepancy: an RMSE between my trajectory and the elite model in metres, and an R² of that model evaluated on my data.
  • An honest account of scale: the size difference between the two swimmers reported as an explicit scale factor rather than absorbed into a scale-invariant score.

3. Strategies for Addressing Personal Engagement (Criterion C)

This criterion is about creative application of mathematics learned in this course.

As a competitive high school swimmer who placed 2nd in the B final at OFSAA and who has coaching experience, I routinely analyse stroke mechanics and drag reduction from recordings. The usual vocabulary for this is qualitative ("catch the water", "finish the pull"), and it is not measurable. What interests me mathematically is that a hand moving through water traces a curve, and that curve can be written as a vector function, differentiated, integrated and compared. The creative step is to treat a coach's cue as a parametric curve and a disagreement between two swimmers as a residual from a fitted model, which converts an argument about style into a number with an uncertainty attached.

If the cubic model proves inadequate, the response will be mathematical rather than ad hoc: raise the degree only where the point count supports it, restrict the window to the phase where a single polynomial is justifiable, and report the change in R² and in arc length so the effect of the modelling choice is visible.

5. Resources / Sources

My footage: two clips of my own competitive 50 m freestyle sprint, filmed from the pool deck at 1920 × 1080, 29.97 fps, and digitised by me one frame at a time (24 points per clip over 0.77 s).
Elite footage: underwater 50 m freestyle race footage of an elite sprinter, 1280 × 720, 60 fps, digitised in the same way (18 points).
Calibration object: a 0.19 m swim cap, measured in several frames of each clip (5-7 frames) to obtain the pixel-to-metre scale and its uncertainty (±14-18%), with the pool's lane markings checked as a cross-reference.
Software: Python with NumPy and pandas for the polynomial fits, numerical differentiation and integration; results reproducible from the digitised coordinates.
